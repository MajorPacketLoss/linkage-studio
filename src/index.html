Quick help

Start here

  1. Open Examples, or draw pins and connect them with links.
  2. Add ground pivots or sliders. Choose a motion input in Properties, then Solve.
  3. Press Play to watch motion. Open an Analysis tab for polygons, results or a motion study.

FAQs

How do I move around or select an area?

Mouse: scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan, left-drag empty grid to select an area.

Touch: drag empty canvas to pan; pinch with two fingers to zoom/pan, including polygons. Double-tap-and-drag or use Box select to select an area. Lift all fingers after a pinch before editing again.

How do I edit a link or undo a change?

Select an object for Properties, or drag it with Select active. Double-click a link dimension to edit its length: Enter applies; Escape cancels. Entering a length locks it; disable Lock length while editing to reshape it.

Delete removes the selection. Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes. Snap toggles grid snapping; constraints still take priority.

Why won’t my mechanism solve or move?

Check ground pivots, slider guides, locked lengths and the motion input. Too few constraints leave motion undetermined; conflicting constraints or a singular pose can stop a solution.

For alignment and angle constraints, During simulation keeps the relation active during motion. Leave it off for a starting-pose constraint.

How do gears, pulleys and ropes connect?

New wheels have a Free centre. In Properties, choose a ground shaft, a joint to follow, or Attach shaft to link. Following a joint allows independent spin; attaching to a link locks their rotation together.

Use Wheel and rope connections for meshes, open/crossed belts or rope constraints. Belts need fixed shafts; ropes use signed slider-travel coefficients. Try the pulley examples first.

How do I read polygons or trace a joint’s path?

In Polygons, select a vector for its explanation or open Step-by-step polygon drawing. Polygon appearance controls lines and arrowheads.

Select a joint → Motion paths: Calculated path is solid and uses the Motion study span/samples; Live trail is dashed and keeps the latest 1,000 positions. Clear one trail in Properties or all with Clear trails. Paths are not saved or printed and reset on Open or Undo/Redo.

How do I save or print to scale?

Save downloads a mechanism JSON file; Open restores it. Save before leaving—drawings are not automatically saved. Results and motion studies can be exported as CSV.

For printing, match paper/orientation, use 100% / Actual size, disable browser headers/footers and measure the 50 mm check line. Printed polygons are always complete.

Where are themes, panels and units?

Appearance sets the theme and colors, saved in this browser. Reset follows your system theme. Screen colors do not change prints; polygon styles do.

Use Properties or Analysis to reopen panels; drag the analysis panel’s top edge to resize it. Length changes display units. Angular input is in degrees; rates use seconds and radians.

What are the keyboard shortcuts?
  • Draw: J Pin · L Link · S Slider · F Ground · T Tracer · G Gear · U Pulley
  • Constrain: M Midpoint · C Coincident · K Collinear · H Horizontal · I Vertical
  • Navigate: V Select · Escape Clear selection · Space Play/pause · Home Fit · +/− Zoom
  • Panels: P Properties · A Analysis · ? Help
  • Files: Ctrl+S Save · Ctrl+O Open · Ctrl+N New
  • History: Ctrl+Z Undo · Ctrl+Shift+Z / Ctrl+Y Redo

Drawing shortcuts pause while typing or using a dialog.

Angle constraint

Counterclockwise from the reference to the directed link, from its first joint to its second. Choose whether this angle only sets the starting pose or remains active during motion.

Rope length constraint

For fixed-direction rope segments: n₁s₁ + n₂s₂ = constant. Travel follows each slider’s positive guide direction. A moving pulley often contributes two strands. This does not infer an arbitrary rope routing.

An associated moving wheel uses a stationary rope strand on its positive-normal side (90 degrees counterclockwise from its guide direction); its rim spin is determined by centre travel. Use the worked moving-pulley example for the routed two-strand system.
