Using Linkage Studio

The grid is the drawing workspace. Use the Properties and Analysis buttons in the top bar to reopen closed panels. Drag the top edge of the analysis panel to resize it, or use its retract button to leave only the tab strip; its tabs contain polygons, results, equations and motion studies.

Choose an example, or place pins and connect them with the Link tool. Ground pins fix their location. A slider pin follows a fixed straight guide. Set a link length in Properties, choose one motion input, then Solve.

Midpoint, coincident and collinear relations snap immediately and stay active during simulation. Alignment and angle constraints have a During simulation switch; use it for a motion constraint, or leave it off to set only the construction pose. Shared pins, tracer attachments, sliders, locked lengths and gear meshes remain mechanical constraints.

New links have an editable length. Entering a length enables its editing lock; turn off “Lock length while editing” to reshape it again. Links remain rigid during motion analysis. The padlock beside a dimension marks a locked length.

Select an object to edit it. Drag pins or links to explore the constrained motion. Delete removes the selection. Ctrl+Z undoes edits; Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes them. Right-drag the grid to pan in any tool. Left-drag empty grid to select an area. Drag an object in Select mode to edit it. Escape clears selection and returns to Select. Save and Open use portable JSON files, including files from earlier versions.

Gear wheels can use a ground shaft, a joint-mounted shaft, or a free centre. In Properties, choose shaft support and Apply. Follow a joint lets the wheel spin independently; Attach shaft to link makes the wheel rotate rigidly with that link. Meshing supports carrier-relative motion, including moving shaft centres. A free centre can leave undetermined motion until more constraints are added.

Define pitch size through module and tooth count, then connect gears with an external mesh, internal mesh or common shaft. Select a link and any of its joints when attaching a rigid shaft. Automatic fit moves an editable second shaft into contact or fits an independent pair’s module to the shaft spacing. The placement setting can instead require existing positions to match. Incompatible connections fail without changing the sketch. Gear outlines are schematic. Ideal no-slip kinematics use pitch circles.

U adds a pulley. Connect two fixed shafts with an open or crossed belt in Wheel and rope connections. Rope constraints relate signed slider travels using strand coefficients; the moving-pulley example explains its two supporting strands. These are ideal, inextensible fixed-direction segments. K applies collinear to two links or a link and joint. Select a tracer-bearing link to dimension tracer offsets, radius and angle; entered offsets remain fixed when the base length changes.

Inspect individual polygon parts by selecting a point or segment. For a manual construction, draw the known vector, extend the perpendicular directions, intersect them, and separate normal and tangential acceleration. Stationary pins and zero vectors share the pole.

Print supports physical scales in millimetres on paper. Choose the same paper and orientation in the browser dialog, use 100% or Actual size, disable headers and footers, and measure the 50 mm check line. Add or remove construction guides, dimensions, annotations and component vectors before printing.

Length units are mm, cm, m, in and ft. Rates use seconds, angular input uses degrees and angular rates use rad/s and rad/s². Analysis covers planar rigid links, fixed guides and ideal gears with grounded or joint-mounted shafts. It stops at unresolved or singular configurations.

Angle constraint

Counterclockwise from the reference to the directed link, from its first joint to its second. Choose whether this angle only sets the starting pose or remains active during motion.

Rope length constraint

For fixed-direction rope segments: n₁s₁ + n₂s₂ = constant. Travel follows each slider’s positive guide direction. A moving pulley often contributes two strands. This does not infer an arbitrary rope routing.

An associated moving wheel uses a stationary rope strand on its positive-normal side (90 degrees counterclockwise from its guide direction); its rim spin is determined by centre travel. Use the worked moving-pulley example for the routed two-strand system.
